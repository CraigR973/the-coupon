# Backfill note — 2-1 Hibs, 8 and 15 August 2026

The written record `docs/BUILD_PLAN.md`'s Batch 68 row requires: **which odds are
evidenced and which, if any, are not.**

The Coupon did not exist when this league played on 8 and 15 August 2026. Its first
stored round is 22 August, and two members' picks were missing from that one. This
records what was written, on whose word, and what could not be recovered.

The run is `apps/api/src/backfill_august_2026.py`.

## Why the odds had to come from outside the product

**odds-api.io cannot supply a retrospective price.** `odds_api.py:494` documents it,
verified 2026-08-04: once a fixture settles, `/odds` returns it with `status: "settled"`,
no `scores` key and no bookmakers, and `/odds/multi` drops it entirely. Both endpoints
carry only what is still priced.

So there was nothing to probe and no budget to spend. The odds are an **input** to this
backfill rather than an output of it — and since a winning pick scores `round(odds × 10)`,
an invented price would be an invented leaderboard position.

## What is evidenced

| Round | Picks | Source | Evidenced? |

| Sat 8 Aug 2026 | 12 | bet365 slip, `Sat 08 Aug 13:45`, £3.50 12-fold | **Yes** — screenshot |
| Sat 15 Aug 2026 | 12 | bet365 slip, £4.00 twelve-fold, settled | **Yes** — screenshot |
| Sat 22 Aug 2026 | 2 | owner, 2026-08-24 | **No** — testimony, not a document |

Selections for both full rounds come from the owner's handwritten coupons for those
weeks; the prices come from the two bet365 slips, which carry the same twelve selections
each.

### The 8 August prices are cross-checked against the slip's own arithmetic

The slip quotes fractions and states a return: £3.50 to return £1,660.24. The twelve
fractions multiply to **474.28**, and 474.28 × £3.50 is **£1,659.99** — a 0.015% gap,
which is bet365's rounding of its own figure.

That check is in `tests/test_backfill_august_2026.py` and it is the one that matters: a
single fraction converted wrongly (19/20 read as 1.90, 8/13 as 1.61) is invisible on
re-reading the screenshot and moves a member's points by a whole unit. A second test
asserts each stored decimal is its own fraction rounded to two places, because the product
alone would survive two errors that cancelled.

Note the stored two-decimal values drift about **1.1% high** against the exact fractions
over twelve legs — `4/6` becomes `1.67` three times, `8/13` becomes `1.62`. That is the
schema's `Numeric(6, 2)`, not an error, and it is why the product check uses the fractions.

### The two unevidenced prices

| Member | Fixture | Pick | Price | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lewis | Everton v Crystal Palace | BTTS Yes | 1.70 | Everton 2–0 → **lost** |
| Josh Caldow | Cove Rangers v Peterhead | Peterhead (away) | 2.25 | Cove Rangers 3–0 → **lost** |

Both are the owner's recollection on 2026-08-24, not a slip.

**Neither can move the leaderboard**, and that is why they were acceptable at all: both
picks lost, so `points_awarded` is 0 whatever the price. Had either won, its price would
have set its points directly and the batch's own rule — the backfill does not proceed on a
guessed odd — would have stopped the run instead.

What they *do* affect is Batch 70's pick-shape figures: cumulative odds, average odds, and
the favourite/longshot split on both members' profiles. Those two figures rest on
testimony. A test pins the fact that the unevidenced prices are exactly the non-scoring
ones, so a later edit cannot quietly turn one into a scoring price.

## One scoreline is also unevidenced by the independent source

| Fixture | Competition | Score | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aberdeen v Dundee, 15 Aug | Scotland – League Cup, Group C | 3–0 | settled bet365 slip **and** owner, 2026-08-24 |

Production holds **zero** finished matches in that competition, and that is not a gap in
the ingestion: no source The Coupon uses carries the Scottish League Cup group stage. The
L4 record already lists `scotland-league-cup-group-c` among the three cups api-football
never resolved a competition id for, and FotMob does not carry it either.

The result itself *is* documented, and by two sources rather than one — the settled slip
prints "Aberdeen 3 Dundee 0" with the leg marked lost, and the owner confirmed the same
score independently on 2026-08-24. So this is the same trade the odds make: a documented
value rather than a guess, and better attested than the two prices above. It is held to a stricter rule than the odds are, though: `KNOWN_SCORES` is consulted
**only** for a fixture the store cannot answer, and the run raises if an entry there would
override one it can. A fallback that can silently outrank real data is a second source of
truth, not a fallback, and a test pins both halves of that.

Walesy's 15 August pick was BTTS Yes, so a 3–0 loses it. Nothing scores off this.

## What was *not* taken on trust

**No outcome was transcribed.** Every pick was written `pending` with no points, then
settled by `settle_gameweek` — the same function the 18:00 sweep calls — against
settlements built from the scorelines already in `matches` from the FotMob ingestion. That
covers **25 of the 26 picks**; the twenty-sixth is the League Cup fixture above.

A rehearsal against production data on 2026-08-24, before anything was written, resolved
all 25 through the real matcher and found **every FotMob scoreline agreeing with the
settled 15 August bet365 slip** on all eleven legs that slip reports — two independent
sources, no disagreement.

That matters because the coupons and the results then come from independent sources: the
owner's screenshots say what was picked, FotMob says what happened. `points_awarded` is
computed by the scoring path rather than copied off a slip, which is the row's own
verification requirement met by construction rather than by a separate check.

The run fails closed. An unknown member, a fixture that matches zero or more than one row,
or a missing scoreline raises before anything is written.

## Two decisions recorded

**Rounds are written with `number = NULL`.** The league's 22 August round is "Gameweek 1"
and members were told so. Numbering these 3 and 4 — which `next_gameweek_number` would do,
since it takes one past the season maximum — puts a later number on an earlier date;
renumbering 22 August would rewrite a name people have already used. The column is nullable
for exactly this case, and nothing keys on it: locking, settlement and scoring all read
instants and status.

**Nicknames were mapped with the owner, not inferred.** The coupons name people as
"Walesy", "Robbo", "Gee" and "Tippen". Ten mapped unambiguously to display names; `Robbo →
Craig` and `Gee → Grant Moore` were left over and confirmed by the owner on 2026-08-24
rather than assumed, because points landing on the wrong member is the error nobody spots
later.
